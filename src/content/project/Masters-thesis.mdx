## Resistive fragment assemblages: How do decentralised web technologies better enable collectivist para-archives?

Completed in 2025 at the Creative Computing Institute, University of the Arts London under the supervision of MJ Hunter Bruggemann. Awarded an MA with Distinction.

## Abstract

Anti-capitalist work in [liberating]() [everyday]() information practise from the goals of [monopolising tech companies]() is spread thin across various industry concerns and [disciplinary hegemonies](). By centering collectivist ideals I appeal to the [digital commons]() and [decentralised web]() to enable particular practices of hyperconnected personal [curation]() that have potential for collectivist resistance. Through analysis of public assemblages of fragmented experience on existing web platforms ([Pinterest]() and [Are.na]()) I'll discuss the benefits that [decentralised]() models and the [semantic web]() could have on extra-institutional knowledge-work. This thesis is also a meta-work of [self-exemplification](). As a living [provotype](), this thesis is presented as part of a personal website indexing [cues]() to my own [self-transcendence]() through collectivist pedagogy and experimental tech.

## Conclusion

Existing platforms allow assemblage of [affective and instrumental fragments]() for [collective future-building]() but deny [autonomy]() over the resulting [para-archives](). By prioritising agency over data, [semantic connections](), and acknowledging  [blurred public-private boundaries](), we can design for externalised selves that are more resilient and representative. Usability and community ethos are major wins for [Are.na](), and [Pinterest]() benefits from sheer quantity of content, but excluding the financial imperative from these projects makes room for more radical freedoms. In this enquiry I’ve demonstrated the value of *[resistive fragment assemblages]()*, well-documented on [Tumblr]() and presented here through [Pinterest]() and [Are.na](). I’ve discussed how they create [collectivist]() [counter-publics]() through [para-archival]() curation, and how a [decentralised](), [semantic]() model could enable them further.
